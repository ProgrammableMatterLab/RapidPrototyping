### Critical Design Review (CDR) 

The Critical Design Review is the final review of the design portion of a project (the 'point of no return'). The CDR is not intended to yield a finished or polished device prototype; its goal is to design, build, and test all major subcomponents, showing preliminary work towards completing all system features.

Its purpose is to fully solidify the design; the core design at the end of the CDR should be frozen. It should report documentation showing your full pass over key design features, including low fidelity mock-ups of assembly with partially working subsystems.
 
Please see below for the sections required.


#### Preliminary designs and low fidelity prototypes

The CDR documentation should show key designs and partially working subsystems to illustrate your progress toward final device completion. You may adapt the below structure as you see fit, and your efforts may not equally cover every suggested deliverable below, but your work should in general document the following:

##### Mechanical systems

[//]: # Document the development of your mechanical systems, centering on: CAD, fabrication, and physical parts, including videos/gifs of working mechanisms.

1. A plain-language description of what your mechanical system prototype accomplishes, identifying its relation to the final build (did you build a full-fidelity prototype of one particularly important sub-assembly or mechanism? did you sketch or design low-fidelity mockups of all parts?).
1. CAD screenshots showing the development of your design, with dimensioning.
1. Video/gifs of any CAD animations. 
1. Photos taken of your fabricated parts, including justification of your use of 3D printing / laser cutting.
1. Photos and video/gifs of any physical parts/mechanisms.   
1. You should discuss the development of your work; your design decisions, things you tried that did or didn't work, helpful resources you used, suprising outcomes etc.

##### Electronics

1. A plain-language description of what your electronics prototype accomplishes, identifying its relation to the final build (did you build all the circuitry of your device or did you focus on one particular circuit? did you breadboard components that you ultimately plan to solder?).
1. A circuit schematic or wiring diagram. 
1. If appropriate, a circuit simulation gif/video from the Falstad simulator.
1. Photos of your breadboarded components and/or soldered circuit board. 
1. Theoretical estimates of peak and nominal current draw, and validation on your physical setup, confirming your power management is appropriate.
1. You should discuss the development of your work; your design decisions, things you tried that did or didn't work, helpful resources you used, suprising outcomes etc.

##### Code & Software

1. A plain-language description of what your code/software prototype accomplishes, identifying its relation to the final build (did you write an initial version of all your code or did you focus on a particularly challenging part? did you just write testing or debugging sketches or did you implement code that will be deployed on your device?).    
1. Pseudocode for your Arduino (or other) code, explaining the purpose of the main variables, functions, and control logic. For Arduino sketches, identify the number of Digital/Analog Inputs and Outputs and what they're used for.
1. If appropriate, screenshots/gifs of your Serial Monitor (or other software) displaying any prototyped functionality or debugging experiments. 
1. You should discuss the development of your work; your design decisions, things you tried that did or didn't work, helpful resources you used, suprising outcomes etc.


##### Critical feature

Previously, in your PDR, you focused on prototyping the critical part(s) of your project, to as high fidelity as possible, to help you determine as early as possible whether to re-scope. This feature may have been related to theory, code, electronics, design/fabrication or integration. 

Provide a brief update on the development of your critical feature and any adjustments made to the project.

##### Integration

1. A plain-language description of what your integration work accomplishes, identifying its relation to the final build (is it the final version of a specific subsystem? is it an initial test to check tolerances and current draw when a motor is integrated into a mechanism? is it a low-fidelity mock-up of the whole device?).
1. Screenshots, photos, and videos/gifs of any assemblies that integrate work from your mechanical, electronics, and programming work. For example, this might be:
    1. A soldered circuit board fastened to/inside a mechanical enclosure.
    1. Electronics being controlled via Arduino.
    1. Etc.   


#### Updated System & Management Plan

1. Provide an updated Gantt chart and comment on any changes from the previous version, identifying any challenges that were overcome or that persist. 
1. Clearly detail the remaining tasks required to complete the final project. 
1. If you plan to deviate from the previously defined project scope or details, provide updated versions of any relevant documentation (System Level Requirements, Performance Requirements, Architecture Block Diagram, BOM) 



### Presentation 

Your CDR should show preliminary work towards completing all major system features, including focused work on mechanical design, electronics, and code, as well as physical mock-ups or partially working subsystems.

Prepare a **6-8 minute presentation of your CDR** to give to the class during lab. We discourage you from presenting slides; instead we encourage you to polish your published CDR on your website and narrate us through that.
