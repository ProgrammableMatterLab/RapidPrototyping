## Course overview

This studio course will introduce students to designing and fabricating electromechanical devices. Lectures will cover key methods and tools required to go from an idea to prototyping a functional electromechanical device. Topics include CAD, 3D printing, Laser cutting, electronic design and breadboarding, and embedded systems programming. Weekly lectures will be supplemented with weekly laboratory exercises, culminating in a final project.

### In-class participation & recordings

This is a hands-on fabrication class, and every week will involve design and physical manufacturing under staff guidance. In-person class participation is therefore required; we do not anticipate recording lectures.


### Learning Objectives

The course will teach students the following:

1. Learn traditional design workflows to create 2D and 3D geometries using CAD.
1. Utilize parametric design to generate 3D geometries.
1. Understand and develop hands-on rapid prototyping skills for using 3D printing and laser cutting to build physical objects.
1. Design circuits that use inputs (sensors) & outputs (actuators & displays).
1. Prototype physical circuits via breadboarding and soldering.
1. Programming in C++ for microcontrollers (Arduino); reading and writing to peripherals.
1. Understand limitations of rapid prototyping, design for manufacturing, and paths to mass manufacturing.
1. Combine electronics, CAD, and programming to build a physical prototype in domains such as robotics, wearables, manufacturing automation, or research.
1. Iterative design and project management to go from your own idea to a functional device.
1. Present final project through live demonstrations, articulating design choices & implementation.

## Grading 

### Grade Allocation

- Weekly assignments & documentation: 40%
- Final project & documentation & presentation: 45%
- Participation & In-class engagement: 15%

### Weekly Assignment grades

- **0-2.9:** incomplete or missing assignment.
- **3:** you completed the minimal in-class example and documented it well.
- **3.35:** you went beyond the minimal assignment and documented it well.
- **3.7:** you made an excellent project with excellent documentation.
- **4:** impressive to the point of overachieving.

Each assignment has a “minimum expectation” required for a grade of 3. Each assignment then has non-exhaustive suggestions that can be completed for “full credit” up to a grade of 4. As these are creative design challenges, it is not possible to prescribe an explicit rubric for these: If one assignment lists 5 suggested full-credit tasks; completing just one difficult task with exceptional quality could suffice for full credit; completing 3 with low quality may as well.

### Weekly assignment Late Policy

The demanding nature and pace of hardware-based studio courses necessitates your regular attendance and requires that deadlines be consistently met. Class material is cumulative, and will involve in-person manufacturing and training with external staff that cannot be re-scheduled: not completing the readings or assignments on time, or missing class, will prevent you from completing lab-work and lead to a poor grade. However, we will still use a uniform late policy to flexibly accommodate late assignments for individual circumstances. We will effectively offer end-of-quarter extensions to every student for two individual assignments, in the sense that your documentation can be published up until the end of the quarter for full credit. What this means is that we will grade assignments when they are due, and note any missing submission; on the day of the final class meeting, we will check whether missing assignments are completed, and grade up to two of them. This will be done automatically; please do not email us requesting extensions. TLDR: 2 assignments can be submitted up to the last day of class without penalty.

### Final Project grade

The final project will use the same grading protocol as the weekly assignments. It should integrate skills developed during the class, incorporating at a minimum:

1. 3D design and fabrication (3D printing and/or laser cutting)
1. Electronics (inputs and outputs)
1. Microcontroller programming

More details on the documentation required and full credit will be given in class, and you can discuss your particular project idea with the instructor to determine its suitability.

### AI policy & use of other software/hardware:

This class will use and provide support for a specific set of hardware and software. However, you are free and encouraged to use any tools available to you, whether AI, or other software, or hardware. For example, you may have already learned a different CAD software you wish to use, or have access to additional fabrication equipment on campus, or you may wish to generate preliminary microcontroller code and circuit schematics using an LLM. This comes with two caveats:

1. You must declare your use of AI or other tools in your documentation for any task you use it for.
1. You must demonstrate understanding of any artifact you wish to receive credit for, meaning you will be expected to be able to explain every line of code or design you present or document.

## Illnesses and other intermittent situations

In the event that the university must shift to virtual instruction, students will be notified via Canvas and lectures will be provided via Zoom. These lectures will be recorded.
