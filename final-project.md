## Final Project Overview

The final project will involve designing, fabricating, assembling, and programming a physical device using the skills learnt throughout the course. It is deliberately open-ended; a non-exhaustive list of possible projects include machines, robots, or kinetic art sculptures. See the **Device** section below for more details on expectations to help inform your project ideas. In groups, you will first formulate project proposals from which to down-select a Final Project with the help of the staff. The following weeks are centered around key milestones to develop and finalize your chosen project.

## Grading


As detailed on the Syllabus, the Final Project as a whole constitutes 45% of the class grade. This is broken down as follows:
1. Physical Device 30%
1. Website documentation 30%
1. Interim presentations 10%
1. Final Presentation & Demo 10%
1. Peer Review 5%
1. Individual Contribution Record 15%

### Physical device

The final project should integrate the core skills developed during the class, incorporating at a minimum:
1. 3D design and fabrication (3D printing and/or laser cutting), including mechanisms
1. Electronics (inputs and outputs)
1. Microcontroller programming
1. Functional behavior demonstrating a clearly defined design objective

For full credit, you can incorporate the following:
1. Demonstrate ambitious technical depth, incorporating independently researched designs or algorithms.
1. Incorporate learnings from other classes, e.g. FEA for part design, control algorithms for motor control, etc.
1. Develop a device that supports solving an open research problem
1. A highly creative and novel prototype that would convincingly solve a problem in the real world (accessibility, environmental monitoring, etc.)  
1. A highly polished device with exceptional visual quality and reliable function. 

You will be able to discuss your project ideas with the staff to determine their suitability.

**A note on Materials/Components**: You will have access to all of the materials and components that have been introduced in the course far. You can also make requests for additional materials and components. To keep this tractable and ensure timely delivery, you will be asked to specify such requests during the CoDR. See the CoDR assignment in [Week 7]({{ '/docs/week7/' | relative_url }}) for details.

### Website documentation


You will create a new Google Site as a group to document your final project. This site will ultimately have 4 subpages; one for each of the 4 major milestones (CoDR, PDR, CDR, and Final Build). Specific deliverables (CAD, block diagrams, videos, pseudocode etc.) that should be documented on the website for each major milestone will be given on the relevant weekly pages. 

Instructions for setting up the Final Project Site are detailed in the assignment for [Week 7]({{ '/docs/week7/' | relative_url }}).

### Interim presentations

In addition to documenting all 4 of the major milestones on your website, you will also _present_ the last 3 of the major milestones to the class (skipping milestone 1: the CoDR). What we call the interim presentations are the PDR and CDR. You will present these Design Reviews to the class, and receive feedback from your peers and the staff. Pay close attention to the deadlines to note when the presentations will occur; some on **Wednesdays**, some on **Fridays**.

### Final presentation & Demo

You will present your final presentation of the last milestone, the Final Build, on the final meeting of class, **Friday, December 11**. Each group will have approximately 10 minutes to present their work and show a live demo of their physical device, plus time for Q&A. The tentative plan for each group is:
1. Presentation (8 minutes)
1. Live demo (2 minutes)
1. Q&A with class (3 minutes)



### Peer Review
In the final week of class, we will distribute a Peer Review as an assignment. Each group member will _individually_ submit a Peer Review describing the contributions of your team members. The Peer Review grade is an _individual_ grade that reflects the _quality_ of the review _you_ write for each of your peers. 

### Individual Contribution Record
The Individual Contribution Record will also be determined by the peer review: it will be based on your _peers'_ assessment of _your_ contribution. Note that in the rare circumstance where there is extreme imbalance between student contributions, we will modify this grade accordingly.
